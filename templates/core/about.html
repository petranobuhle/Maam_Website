Youth mental health awareness & advocacy

Beyond the Silence begins with listening.

MAAM is a youth-led mental health awareness and advocacy initiative, beginning with young people in urban Zimbabwe and making room for the diversity of African experiences.

Why MAAM exists

People deserve to be heard, understood and connected to support.

Mental health challenges can be harder to navigate when people encounter:

  • Stigma and discrimination.
  • Misconceptions about mental health.
  • Fear of judgement or unwanted exposure.
  • Limited awareness of where appropriate support may be found.
  • Difficulty having open conversations about emotional wellbeing.

Speaking about a need can matter, but the responsibility is not on a person to find perfect words. Communities share responsibility for listening with compassion and creating safe, respectful spaces.

The meaning behind our name

Mwana Asingachemi Anofira Mumbereko

“The baby that does not cry will die in the cradle.”

This Shona proverb speaks to how expressing a need can help someone be noticed and find support. For MAAM, it also carries a responsibility for the people around them: to listen, respond with compassion and help create spaces where it feels safer to speak.

The proverb is not a reason to blame anyone for distress, silence or difficulty seeking help. Each person’s circumstances are different, and support should not depend on someone having the “right” words.

Our direction

Awareness with compassion. Support with dignity.

Our mission

Make understanding and support easier to reach.

Promote mental health awareness, challenge stigma, encourage open and compassionate conversations, and connect people with credible information and appropriate support.

Our vision

Communities where no one has to feel ashamed to seek support.

Communities where mental health is understood, conversations are welcomed, people are treated with dignity, and seeking support is not a source of shame.

Our objectives

Practical steps toward a more supportive culture.

MAAM’s objectives are to:

  • Increase mental health literacy and awareness.
  • Challenge stigma and harmful misconceptions.
  • Encourage supportive conversations among young people and communities.
  • Share credible educational resources.
  • Connect visitors with legitimate mental health organisations and services.
  • Promote responsible storytelling, consent and privacy.
  • Encourage community participation and advocacy.

How MAAM intends to work

A connected approach, built around people.

These are the initiative’s intended workstreams, not claims that programmes or partnerships have already taken place.

01

Education and awareness

Make clear, accessible mental health information easier to understand and share.

02

Community voices

Make room for experiences and stories shared voluntarily, with consent and privacy treated with care.

03

Campaigns and advocacy

Challenge stigma and harmful misconceptions, and encourage more supportive community responses.

04

Credible resources

Share useful materials with clear sources and respect for their creators and intended use.

05

Support directory

Help visitors identify organisations and services, while encouraging them to confirm current availability directly.

06

Participation and partnerships

Invite community participation and approach potential partnerships responsibly. No partnership is claimed here.

Leadership and boundaries

Clear about who we are—and what we are not.

Public-facing project role: MAAM Project Lead. No personal name is published.

MAAM is an awareness and advocacy initiative, not a counselling centre, mental health clinic or healthcare provider. It does not provide diagnosis, therapy, medical treatment or emergency response.

MAAM is still being developed. This page describes its purpose and intended work, not completed programmes, impact figures or established partnerships. No unconfirmed board, clinical team or registration status is claimed.
